How mutual funds work

Many investors put money into funds without knowing what happens next. Here is the short version.

Pooling

Many investors put money into one fund. A fund manager invests that pool in shares, bonds or other assets.

NAV

The Net Asset Value is the price of one unit. It changes every day with the value of what the fund holds.

SIP

A Systematic Investment Plan invests a fixed amount every month, so you do not need to time the market.

Costs

Every fund charges an expense ratio. Regular plans include distributor commission. Direct plans do not. Ask about this before investing.

Why investors choose mutual funds

Regulated

Funds operate under SEBI rules, with regular disclosure of holdings and costs.

Diversified

One fund holds many securities, which spreads risk compared with a few individual shares.

Flexible

Start with a modest SIP or a lump sum, and in most open-ended funds you can redeem when you need to.

Not risk-free

Values rise and fall, and returns are not assured. Time horizon and suitability matter more than any single fund.
